Introduction

What is a public-private attributed graph?

A public-private graph is a network where some connections are visible to everyone, while others are visible only to specific users. Different users therefore have different personalized views of the same platform.

Why community search on public-private graphs?

  • Real social networks are partially observable: not all friendships are public.
  • Using public structure together with user-specific private edges and attributes yields more accurate, personalized communities.

Research Problem

Given an attributed public-private graph and a query vertex, find a community containing the query under the querier’s view (public edges plus that user’s private edges/attributes). The community should satisfy a k-core constraint and maximize shared attributes (ACS-PP).

Desiderata. Query participation · private relevance · high attribute correlation · structural cohesiveness (k-core).

Method Overview

Phase-I: Attribute selection via PP-FP-trees.

We first extract the largest set of common attributes from our constructed PP-FP-trees, where a possible candidate subgraph of k nodes associated with such attributes exists.

Phase-II: Candidate subgraph expansion in public graph.

Taking the attributes from Phase-I, we expand the candidate graph locally in public graph by involving those new nodes that have the coreness of at least k.

Phase-III: Public-private community validation.

Finally, we validate the candidate subgraph peeled by the core decomposition algorithm to meet the requirements of k-core and common attributes.

Challenges

Challenge I: Inconsistency between Structure and Attributes.

Structural cohesiveness and shared attributes may be independent of each other in the community model, requiring joint consideration.

Challenge II: Heterogeneous Personalized Graphs.

Private edges and attributes vary across different users, leading to diverse graph views.

Challenge III: Expensive Indexing in Large-Scale Graphs.

Constructing and maintaining indexes for each personalized graph is computationally expensive in large networks.

Conclusion

We formulate and study the ACS-PP problem to find cohesive communities containing query nodes over public-private graphs. We develop an index-based framework based on public-private frequent pattern trees, which can support the fast search of common attributes. Experiments demonstrate its effectiveness and efficiency against state-of-the-art competitors.
